# Phantom Scan: Ready-Made CSI Datasets and the RuView Model — What Exists and What Works on Our ESP32-S3 Rig

**Short answer: No.** We found no public dataset that we can drop into `data/datasets/<room>/` and train our per-room activity classifier or people-count network on without re-recording. RuView publishes model weights but no usable labelled training set. Its presence head is mathematically unable to output "absent".\[1\] The closest public data (WiCount, WiFall, RF_ESP32_Dataset, Less-is-More, TU Wien ESP32) comes from other rooms, other rigs and other subjects, and several of these sets have restrictive or missing licences. They are useful for pretraining, testing our pipeline and studying methods. They are not a replacement for our own recordings.

## TL;DR

- **No drop-in dataset exists for our rig, rooms or labels.** The closest matches are RS2002 WiCount (people count 0–3) and WiFall (fall, jump, sit, stand, walk). Both use single-antenna ESP32-S3 at 2.4 GHz, 20 MHz, 52 subcarriers and about 100 Hz. But each was recorded in one meeting room, neither states a licence, and neither has our empty_fan or "active" classes. Our protocol already says models do not transfer between rooms, rigs and subjects, so these sets can only be used for pretraining and benchmarking.
- **Do not deploy RuView's model as-is, even as advisory output.**
  - RuView trained its 2.4 GHz encoder on one overnight capture: one room, two nodes, 6,062 of 6,063 frames labelled "present".\[2\]
  - The published presence head has bias 8.19 against weight norm 3.67, so p(present) ≥ 0.989 for every possible input (issue #1521).\[1\]
  - The pose models are either CC BY-NC (MM-Fi, 5 GHz, 3 antennas) or self-described as weak (pose_v1: PCK@20 3.0%, runtime stub).\[3\]\[4\]
- **Plan to record our own data in each deployment room.** Use the 128-dim encoder idea, or CC BY 4.0 ESP32 data (Less-is-More, RF_ESP32_Dataset, OpenCSI), only for unsupervised pretraining or feature-extractor benchmarking. Our own recordings must cover 7 labels × ≥5 min × ≥3 sessions on different days × ≥2 subjects, plus empty and empty_fan negatives.

## Key Findings

### Part A – RuView and its published artefacts

**A1. Is the raw training data published? Effectively no, and what exists is not usable.**
- The Hugging Face card names the training capture as `data/recordings/overnight-1775217646.csi.jsonl` with "6,063 feature frames". It calls it a "local capture" and gives a repro command that runs against that path in the GitHub repo.\[2\]
- The card describes it as "an overnight capture of one sleeping person: **6,062 of 6,063** frames are labelled 'present', 1 is 'absent'."\[2\]
- The same card's "Training Details" section claims "60,630 samples from 2 ESP32-S3 nodes over 8 hours". Issue #1521 states that `training-metrics.json` "lists one file, and its span is ~51 minutes".\[1\]\[2\] These figures conflict. The "60,630 samples" may reflect the card's stated "10x" augmentation of 6,063 frames, but that is my inference.\[2\]
- The #1521 reporter re-ran the protocol "from the published weights and recording".\[1\] This suggests the file is reachable from the repo. I could not open it, so its size and exact schema are not verified. Its licence would be the repo licence: the README says MIT, while a release gist says "MIT OR Apache-2.0".\[5\]\[6\] This conflict should be resolved before any redistribution.
- **No labelled multi-class RuView dataset was found.** The card itself says multi-class, multi-room labelled data is something "we don't yet have for this 2.4 GHz feature".\[2\]
- Separately, an external user reported (issue #1703) that dashboard recordings contained "sensing_update events with empty CSI".\[34\] Even RuView's own recording path has been unreliable for producing training files.

**A3. Is there a ready labelled presence, activity or people-count dataset on ESP32 in RuView? No.**
- RuView ships *tools* for us to record our own data: a `train_`-prefixed recording convention for its adaptive classifier, `record-csi-udp.py`, and `collect-training-data.py`.\[4\]
- RuView also ships loaders for MM-Fi and Wi-Pose, which resample to a canonical 56 subcarriers.\[4\]\[36\]\[37\]
- It ships no labelled ESP32 dataset. The user guide lists `cog-person-count/count_v1` as "Real & validated", but I did not find its training data or licence (not verified).\[4\]
- The owner himself told a user (issue #281) that person counting is "Low-Moderate", "Requires 2+ nodes; single-node sees multipath 'phantoms'".\[38\]

**A4. Provenance of `pose_v1.safetensors`: traced.**
- It is committed in the RuView repo at `v2/crates/cog-pose-estimation/cog/artifacts/pose_v1.safetensors`.\[4\]
- The crate docs say it was "produced by the Candle training run on ruvultra's RTX 5080".\[39\]
- RuView labels it "Real but weak": PCK@20 = 3.0% and PCK@50 = 18.5% on a 217-sample holdout, input `[N, 56, 20]` amplitude. Its runtime path "is still a centred-skeleton stub returning confidence=0 — the weights are not yet wired in".\[4\]\[40\]
- The training data behind it: **not found**. Its licence would follow the RuView repo (MIT, or MIT/Apache-2.0 per the conflicting statements). A crate-specific licence was not verified.
- RuView's own docs concede that "a single-antenna, 56-subcarrier CSI stream at a 20-frame window does *not* carry the fine-grained spatial information the multi-antenna NIC research relies on".\[41\] This confirms pose is out of scope for our hardware.

### Part B – Public datasets: what the evidence says

- **Espressif-hardware datasets exist, but none match our labels and room.**
  - WiCount, WiFall and WiGesture share our chip (ESP32-S3), band (2.4 GHz), bandwidth (20 MHz), single antenna and an LLTF-style 52-subcarrier layout.\[7\]\[8\]\[9\]\[12\]\[42\] They are the most *signal-compatible* data available.
  - WiCount's counts 0–3 map onto our 0 / 1 / 2+ classes.\[7\]\[8\]
  - WiFall's walk / sit / stand roughly map onto our walking / still. Its "jump" might proxy "active" (my inference).\[9\]
  - There is no fan/HVAC negative control in any of them.
  - All three are single meeting-room sets with **no licence** on the card.\[8\]\[10\]\[12\]\[42\] Under default copyright, no licence means no grant of reuse rights. This matters for any humanitarian deployment.
- **Less is More (CC BY 4.0) is the largest permissive ESP32 set.** It has 312 h, 45.7 GB, presence/activity/occupancy labels and camera ground truth.\[14\]\[15\]
  - The authors' own results are weak: a best single-link AUC of 0.541, and only 38% detection of sedentary presence.\[14\]
  - A successor repo states that the follow-up paper "revisits that question at equal airtime and with sound evaluation, and finds the opposite", and explains "why the preprint's apartment recording is not used".\[43\] Treat this recording and its conclusions with caution.
- **TU Wien ESP32 through-wall** is the only public ESP32 NLOS (through-wall) set we found, which is relevant to search and rescue. But it is "non-commercial research purposes only", has a single subject, and ships pre-computed PNG spectrograms.\[16\]
- **The Gupta et al. ESP32 multi-person HAR set** is CC BY 4.0, but it is a paywalled IEEE DataPort "Standard Dataset". Its subcarrier layout, rate and antennas are not stated on the landing page.\[13\]
- **Intel 5300 / Atheros / Broadcom sets** (MM-Fi, Wi-Pose, Widar 3.0, XRF55, Meneghello) are 5 GHz and/or MIMO, 30–242 subcarriers, and often 40–80 MHz.\[23\]\[25\]\[30\]\[32\]\[44\] Their spatial (antenna-pair) and bandwidth information does not exist in our 1-antenna, 20 MHz stream, so the models do not transfer. Several are also CC BY-NC.
- **EHUNAM** is the strongest non-ESP32 people-counting set (1–8 people, 8 scenarios, 23 days). Its 2.4 GHz 20 MHz subsets are the most relevant parts. The article (de Armas et al., Scientific Data 12:1950, 17 Dec 2025, doi 10.1038/s41597-025-06238-4) is CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, and the authors' GitHub states "The dataset is open access". The figshare data licence itself could not be verified and must be checked before use.

## Details

### Why the Intel/Atheros sets do not transfer (inference grounded in the sources' specs)
- **Antennas.** MM-Fi uses 3 antenna pairs,\[3\] and Wi-Pose and Widar use 3×3.\[30\]\[44\]\[45\] Models built on those exploit inter-antenna amplitude and phase structure, which our single antenna cannot provide. RuView's own SISO note says the same thing for pose.\[41\]
- **Band and bandwidth.** These sets use 5 GHz at 40–80 MHz. At 20 MHz on 2.4 GHz, our subcarrier spacing, multipath resolution and Fresnel-zone geometry all differ.
- **Subcarrier mapping.** Resampling 30, 114 or 242 subcarriers down to our 51, as RuView does to 56, makes tensors line up. It does not make the physics line up.
- **Labels.** These sets cover gestures, pose and 55 actions. None has fan negatives, and few have true empty-room classes. Meneghello's "E empty room" and EHUNAM's "E" are the exceptions.\[21\]\[23\]

### Format conversion to our JSONL (what would be needed)
- **RS2002 (WiCount / WiFall / WiGesture), CSV, 104 ints = 52 complex values.**\[8\]
  1. Compute amplitude √(re² + im²) per subcarrier.
  2. Map their 52 to our 51 LLTF indices. This requires confirming their subcarrier ordering against the ESP-IDF CSI layout; it is not documented on the cards.
  3. Resample from ~100 Hz to ~67 Hz, or train rate-invariant. CSI-BERT2 itself tests 100 Hz vs 50 Hz mixes.\[7\]\[8\]
  4. Write `{"type":"frame","t":<ms>,"n":<rx id>,"rssi":<rssi>,"a":[51]}` with a header carrying `room = "rs2002_meeting_room"`, `label = <count or action>` and `session = <file>`.
  The receiver count per sample is not stated on the cards, so the node mapping needs checking in the CSV metadata columns.
- **Less is More**, binary I/Q packets (magic byte 0xC5, 52 subcarrier I/Q pairs per the paper): parse, compute amplitude, keep one link per "node", and note the low per-link rate.\[14\]
- **RF_ESP32_Dataset**, 64 subcarriers from esp-csi: select the 51 LLTF bins exactly as our firmware does.\[17\] This is the most direct mapping because it uses the same tool family.
- **EHUNAM / Meneghello / MM-Fi**, .mat: complex CFR matrices.\[21\]\[23\]\[25\] Amplitude plus subcarrier decimation is possible, but only for encoder-pretraining experiments.
- **TU Wien**: PNG spectrograms only.\[16\] Raw CSI cannot be recovered, so the set is only usable if we replicate their spectrogram pipeline.
- **Keep provenance.** Store converted sets under `data/external/<dataset>/`, never under `data/datasets/<room>/`. Tag every header with the source licence so they cannot leak into per-room training or evaluation.

## Recommendations

### C1. Ranking (best use first)
1. **Our own recordings in each deployment room.** This is the only route to a per-room activity or people-count model we can honestly evaluate.
2. **RS2002 WiCount + WiFall:** best signal match (ESP32-S3, 2.4 GHz, 1 antenna, 20 MHz).\[8\]\[42\] Use them for pipeline testing, feature-extractor benchmarking and a pretraining experiment. Do not ship weights trained on them until the authors grant a licence; email the listed contact.
3. **RF_ESP32_Dataset (CC BY 4.0):** closest rig (TP-Link AP → single-antenna ESP32, 64 subcarriers, 8 environments).\[17\] Good for cross-room robustness tests and self-supervised pretraining.
4. **Less is More (CC BY 4.0):** large, permissive, presence/occupancy.\[15\] Use it for unsupervised encoder pretraining and studying fan-free empty baselines. Note the successor's disavowal of the recording.\[43\]
5. **EHUNAM 2.4 GHz subsets:** people-count method study up to 8 people.\[21\] Licence check required.
6. **TU Wien ESP32:** through-wall method study only (non-commercial).\[16\]
7. **Gupta et al.:** only if someone has an IEEE DataPort subscription.\[13\]
8. **MM-Fi, Wi-Pose, Widar 3.0, XRF55, Meneghello:** literature context only. Do not train on them for our rig.

**Blunt answer to "can we skip re-recording?": No.** No dataset matches our room, node geometry, channel 2, ~67 fps self-ping traffic, 51-bin LLTF layout, or our label set (especially empty_fan, active and three_people).

### C2. Using RuView's model without our data
- **Presence head:** do not use, even as advisory output. It is provably constant ("present" ≥ 0.989).\[1\] An advisory that always says "someone is here" is actively harmful in search and rescue, because it masks true empties and wastes responder time.
- **Encoder (csi-embed-v2):** technically loadable (MIT). But it consumes RuView's 8 engineered features at 1 Hz, not our 51 amplitudes,\[2\] so we would have to re-implement their feature extractor exactly. It was trained on one sleeping person in one room. Treat it at most as an experimental embedding to compare against our own baseline features, and only after a head is trained on *our* labelled data.
- **Risks to record in the project log:**
  - Licence ambiguity: MIT vs "MIT OR Apache-2.0"; mmfi-pose is CC BY-NC 4.0.\[3\]\[5\]\[6\]
  - Provenance: one capture; conflicting 8 h vs ~51 min descriptions; a malformed safetensors header (#1522); previously misreported metrics (#882, #1879).\[1\]\[2\]
  - Domain mismatch: rig, room, rate and features all differ.
  - Public credibility disputes: a Cybernews article ("Viral GitHub project claims WiFi can 'see through walls' – developers aren't convinced") quotes Hacker News user luketaylor saying "This whole repository is a bunch of vibe-coded boilerplate that doesn't include almost any of the core things it claims to do", and a fork labelled "SCAM ALERT" alleges fake data. The latter is partisan and predates later fixes, but it is still a reputational risk if we cite RuView numbers.
- **Pose (mmfi-pose, wiflow-v1, pose_v1):** out of scope. They require MIMO or camera supervision, carry non-commercial terms (mmfi-pose), or are self-reported at 3.0% PCK@20 with a stub runtime (pose_v1).\[3\]\[35\]\[40\]

### C3. Specific secondary uses
- **Encoder pretraining (self-supervised, contrastive or masked):** Less is More, RF_ESP32_Dataset, OpenCSI, and WiCount/WiFall pending licence. Only ESP32 2.4 GHz HT20 data should be used, converted to our 51-bin amplitude JSONL. Fine-tune and evaluate exclusively on our rooms.
- **Feature-extractor benchmarking:** run our variance, Doppler and FFT features on WiCount (count 0–3) and WiFall (walk/sit/stand) as sanity checks of the code path, not as accuracy claims.
- **Fall detection study:** WiFall (ESP32-S3, 10 subjects; labels are per recording without fall onset),\[9\]\[11\] EHUNAM MC1 "F" falls, and our own staged falls on a mat if we add a fall label later.\[11\]\[21\]
- **Through-wall effects:** TU Wien DP_NLOS/DA_NLOS (5 rooms, non-commercial).\[16\] Use it to set expectations for NLOS degradation before field trials.
- **Multi-node fusion design:** Less is More, read together with its successor repo, as a warning that naive feature concatenation across many links can hurt.\[14\]\[43\] This is relevant to how we fuse our 4 nodes.

### C4. What we must record ourselves (minimum, per deployment room)
- **Labels:** empty, empty_fan (fan/HVAC on, room empty), still (1 person), walking, active, two_people, three_people.
- **Quantity:** ≥5 min per label per session, ≥3 sessions on different days, ≥2 subjects (more for people-count), all in the deployment room with the final node placement on channel 2.
- **Negatives:** long empty and empty_fan blocks, including overnight, so we avoid exactly RuView's single-class failure.
- **Session hygiene:** keep sessions separate in JSONL headers. Use day-level (session-level) train/test splits, not random frame splits; Less is More's forward-chaining day splits are a good template.\[14\] Log fps per node (expected ~67/s since the 2026-11-09 channel-2 change). Keep Oct 2026 data (43–65 fps) as a separate domain.
- **Ground truth:** a contemporaneous label log (or a short camera reference used only for labelling, then deleted), plus subject IDs for leave-one-subject-out testing.
- **Vitals:** do not train or claim vitals unless we add a reference sensor (chest strap or similar) and validate it separately.

## Caveats
- **Accuracy figures** in this report (RuView 82.3%, 82.69%, 92.9%, 2.5%, 3.0%; Less is More AUC 0.541; EHUNAM ">90%") are the sources' own claims. None has been verified by us, and none may be quoted as Phantom Scan performance.
- **Not verified:**
  - the overnight JSONL's actual size, schema and presence in the repo;
  - the cog-person-count/count_v1 data and licence;
  - the Wi-Pose licence;
  - the EHUNAM figshare data licence;
  - the RS2002 receiver count and subcarrier ordering;
  - TU Wien antenna configuration;
  - RF_ESP32_Dataset and OpenCSI label classes;
  - Gupta et al. subcarrier and rate details.
- **RS2002 card sources:** the WiCount and WiFall card details were read from Hugging Face pull-request branches (`refs/pr/2`, `refs/pr/1`). The live main-branch cards may differ slightly. The WiFall card text is self-described as AI-generated.\[7\]\[9\]
- **RuView pages:** I could not fetch the RuView GitHub pages directly. Repo claims come from Hugging Face cards, GitHub issue #1521 (fetched in full), docs.rs, and search-indexed copies of the README and user guide. Those may lag the current main branch.
- **Conflicts noted, not resolved:** RuView training span (8 h vs ~51 min); RuView repo licence (MIT vs MIT/Apache-2.0); MM-Fi action count (27 vs 25); Less is More sampling rate (~10 Hz per link vs 1.4 Hz cycle);\[14\]\[15\] mmfi-pose cross-environment figure (17.5% card vs ~11.6% gist).\[1\]\[2\]\[3\]\[26\]\[27\]\[46\]
