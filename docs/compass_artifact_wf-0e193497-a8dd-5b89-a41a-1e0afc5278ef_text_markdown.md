# CNN-LSTM Deep Learning for Wi-Fi CSI Human Motion/Presence Detection: Applicability to "Phantom Scan"

## TL;DR
- **A CNN-LSTM (or CNN-GRU / attention-augmented) classifier on the two-node, 64-subcarrier, single-antenna CSI amplitude stream is a well-supported, high-value upgrade** to Phantom Scan's amplitude-variance heuristic for the *motion/presence* task — single-link systems in the literature reach 92–98% for presence and human-vs-non-human detection — but published headline accuracies are systematically inflated by data-leakage (subject-mixed splits) and in-domain testing, so the team should budget for ~10–30 percentage-point drops in realistic cross-subject/cross-environment deployment.
- **The single-antenna constraint is NOT a blocker** for motion/presence detection or single-person vital-sign extraction — the strongest comparable systems (Pulse-Fi heart-rate; Wi-MoID / SrcSense / "Who Moved My Cheese?" human-vs-non-human; single-link ACF motion detection) all run on one Tx-Rx link — but it *does* preclude AoA/localization-grade accuracy, and the team's ±2 BPM respiration / ±5 BPM heart-rate / multi-target targets are only partly achievable, and only under near-line-of-sight, low-noise conditions.
- **Deploy the model on the PC/edge server, not on the ESP32-S3.** A full CNN-LSTM belongs on the PC; only a heavily quantized 1D-CNN/tiny-GRU is realistic on-node via TensorFlow Lite Micro. Critically, the model must feed a *confidence scalar* into Phantom Scan's sovereign `is_human` verdict, never override it — this is also the correct engineering choice given the documented vibration/non-human false-trigger risk.

## Key Findings

**1. CNN-LSTM is a mature, benchmarked architecture family for CSI human sensing, but the "best" model is task-dependent.** Published hybrids include LSTM-CNN (Shang et al., ~94% multi-activity), CNN+GRU, ConvLSTM/DeepConvLSTM, attention-BiLSTM (ABLSTM, ≥95%), CNN-ABLSTM, and Transformer variants (WiSigPro). The SenseFi benchmark (Yang et al., *Patterns* 4(3):100703, 2023) found no single architecture dominates: "On UT-HAR, ResNet-18 exhibits the best accuracy of 98.11%, followed by CNN-5… The BiLSTM yields the best performance on two NTU-Fi benchmarks." Importantly for Phantom Scan, SenseFi notes shallow CNN-5, GRU, LSTM and CNN+GRU are all "small and acceptable for model inference in the edge." Phantom Scan does not need an exotic architecture; a compact CNN+GRU/CNN-LSTM is defensible.

**2. Single-link/single-antenna high accuracy is real and directly comparable to Phantom Scan.** Pulse-Fi (Kocheta, Bhatia & Obraczka, UC Santa Cruz, IEEE IoTDCOSS 2025) used two ESP32s — each with a single antenna, 64 subcarriers, 20 MHz, amplitude-only — and a compact LSTM to estimate heart rate. The paper's results tables report MAE 0.46 BPM at a 30 s window on the locally collected ESP-HR-CSI dataset (7 participants) and 0.14 BPM on the 118-participant EHealth dataset; the project's summary headline cites an best-case MAE as low as 0.08 BPM with models that "run locally real-time on the ESP32 chips (<600KB of RAM)." (Treat the 0.08 BPM figure as the best-case headline; the 0.46/0.14 values are the per-dataset table results.) Pulse-Fi explicitly discarded phase: "We chose not to use phase information as Pulse-Fi employs low-cost, single-antenna devices and thus does not have access to phase difference information." Its pipeline is amplitude conversion → DC removal → 3rd-order Butterworth 0.8–2.17 Hz (48–130 BPM) → Savitzky-Golay → overlapping-window segmentation → LSTM → dense. **This is the single most architecturally similar published system to Phantom Scan's current hardware.**

**3. Human-vs-non-human / vibration discrimination is a solved-enough problem on a single link — and it is largely NOT done with CNN-LSTM.** The Origin Wireless / University of Maryland lineage — "Who Moved My Cheese? Human and Non-human Motion Recognition with WiFi" (Zhu, Wu, Zeng, Wang, Liu, 2022 IEEE 19th MASS, pp. 476–484), "Wi-MoID: Human and Nonhuman Motion Discrimination Using WiFi With Edge Computing" (Zhu, Hu, Wang, Wu, Zeng, Liu, IEEE IoT Journal 11(8):13900–13912, 2024), and its deep-learning successor SrcSense (transfer-learned ResNet-50) — distinguishes humans from pets, robot vacuums, and fans on a single Tx-Rx link, through walls, using the **autocorrelation function (ACF) of CSI power and a statistical-EM motion model**, not raw-CSI deep nets. Reported: "Who Moved My Cheese?" 97.7% accuracy / 95.7% true-positive rate / 95.2% unseen-environment (Gaussian SVM); Wi-MoID 97.34% accuracy / 1.75% false-alarm / 95.98% unseen; SrcSense 95.84% validation / 91.71% unseen / 99.77% with 20 s of accumulation. The key lesson: an oscillating fan is periodic and separable, but pets are the residual hard case. This directly validates Phantom Scan's decision to keep a physics-based verdict and use ML only to adjust confidence.

**4. Through-wall (incl. drywall) at 2.4 GHz is feasible but degrades in NLoS, and reported figures need scrutiny.** Adib & Katabi's WiSee/See-Through-Walls (SIGCOMM 2013) established feasibility; commodity-WiFi through-wall HAR papers report ~90–97.5% (an RNN-LSTM reaching 97.5% on seven activities) but on in-domain splits. Strohmayer & Kampel (ESP32-S3 + 2.4 GHz biquad antenna, 18.5 m across five rooms, CNN on CSI spectrograms) demonstrated feasibility and released a *small* dataset (392 LoS / 384 NLoS spectrograms, 3 classes). Nakamura et al.'s CSI-spectrogram fall detection reached ~90–96% (ResNet-based). A single-link ESP32 replication of that pipeline warned that activity energy was "still not clearly distinguishable from the noise," underscoring that ESP32 CSI quality is materially worse than the Intel 5300 NIC data behind most high-accuracy papers. Factors that most affect accuracy: distance & Fresnel-zone geometry, wall material/thickness (drywall favorable; concrete/metal severe), subcarrier count, sampling rate, and antenna count.

**5. The data-leakage problem is severe and pervasive in this literature.** Varga's "Critical Analysis of Data Leakage in WiFi CSI-Based HAR Using CNNs" (Sensors 2024, 24(10):3159), "Why Partitioning Matters" (Signals 2025, 6(4):59) and the companion Inventions 2024, 9(4):90 showed that widely cited 99%+ CSI-HAR results collapse under subject-separated splits — "the original evaluation relied on random data splitting without subject separation, causing substantial data leakage and inflated results," with original "precision rates of 95% or higher" showing "substantial decline" once individuals are properly separated. Cross-domain drops are large: Widar3.0 (Zheng et al., MobiSys 2019 / TPAMI) reports 92.7% in-domain vs 82.6–92.4% cross-domain (89.7% cross-location, 82.6% cross-orientation, 92.4% cross-environment), and WiGr drops below 20% at a new location. Any Phantom Scan accuracy claim must use subject- and environment-separated evaluation or it will not be credible to the JODDB/civil-defence stakeholder.

**6. Edge/latency and TFLM-on-ESP32 feasibility.** Compact CNN-LSTM/DeepConvLSTM models are small: an INT8-quantized DeepConvLSTM HAR model was 136.51 KB with 21 ms inference on MCU-class hardware. TFLM added a `UNIDIRECTIONAL_SEQUENCE_LSTM` op in Sept 2022, so small LSTMs *can* run on MCUs, but an unrolled LSTM needing ~398 KB RAM failed to load on a 256 KB-class board — ESP32-S3's ~512 KB SRAM (+PSRAM) makes a tiny model borderline-feasible but risky. A separate ESP32-S3 embedded HAR study (DenseNet variant, EMD augmentation) achieved 92.43% at 232 ms *on-node*. Verdict: run the real model on the PC; reserve TFLM-on-node only for a trivial motion gate.

**7. Data requirements and small-team mitigation.** CNN-LSTM CSI models are data-hungry and environment-fragile. Documented mitigations that fit a small academic project: temporal-window shift, time-warping, amplitude scaling, Gaussian-noise injection, EMD-based augmentation (one embedded study raised accuracy from 59.91% to 97.55% via EMD augmentation), GAN synthesis (CsiGAN, CSI4Free), diffusion-augmented CSI, contrastive/few-shot learning, and transfer learning from pre-trained backbones (SenseFi showed pre-training stabilizes convergence). But note the "dilution effect" (Khamaisi & Rodrigues, 2026): in a 72-link ESP32-C3 mesh, a single well-placed link *outperformed* multi-link fusion (AUC 0.541 vs 0.489) — directly relevant to Phantom Scan's two-node fusion plan.

## Details

### Q1 — Published CNN-LSTM & hybrid architectures
- **LSTM-CNN (Shang et al., J. Phys. Conf. Ser. 1883:012139, 2021):** LSTM for temporal dependencies + CNN for spatial features; 94.14% average multi-activity accuracy; beat standalone LSTM.
- **ABLSTM (Chen et al., "WiFi CSI Based Passive HAR Using Attention Based BLSTM"):** bidirectional LSTM over raw sequential CSI + attention weighting; ≥95% on StanWiFi; landmark, widely reproduced, compute-heavy.
- **CNN-ABLSTM:** CNN front-end + attention BiLSTM to cut complexity while keeping robustness.
- **STC-NLSTMNet (Sensors/PMC9823549):** depthwise-separable conv + feature-attention + nested LSTM; 98.20% (Multi-environment) and 99.88% (StanWiFi) — in-domain, likely leakage-affected.
- **CNN-LSTM-Attention with 2×2 array ESP32 (MDPI Electronics 14:1594, 2025):** ~98%, but uses an antenna array (not Phantom Scan's config).
- **CNN+GRU vs BiLSTM (Wakili et al., arXiv 2506.11165):** CNN+GRU 95.20% on UT-HAR (BiLSTM only 66.80% there); BiLSTM 92.05% on NTU-Fi — the CNN front-end matters when temporal resolution is low.
- **DeepCount (arXiv 1903.05316):** CNN+LSTM crowd counting, 86.4% up to 5 people — multi-target is materially harder.
- **Transformer/attention:** WiSigPro (multi-head attention) and ViT variants; strong cross-domain potential, higher training cost.

Input tensor shapes are typically: (a) 2D "CSI image"/spectrogram [subcarriers × time] to a CNN; (b) sequence [time steps × subcarrier-features] to an LSTM/GRU; or (c) stacked [time × subcarriers × channels(amp/phase)] for a ConvLSTM.

### Q2 — Single-antenna / single-link input
- **Pulse-Fi (UCSC 2025):** single antenna, amplitude-only, compact LSTM (LSTM → dense → ReLU), 30 s windows; MAE 0.46 BPM (ESP32) / 0.14 BPM (EHealth, 118 subjects), best-case headline 0.08 BPM, running in <600 KB RAM. Consistent across 1–3 m; explicitly beats multi-antenna SOTA.
- **Single-link ACF motion detection (WiDetect lineage)** and Wi-MoID/SrcSense show single-link suffices for motion and human-vs-non-human.
- **SA-WiSense / RespirFi (arXiv 2025):** address single-antenna respiration "blind spots" — confirming single-antenna respiration works but is location-sensitive and needs blind-spot mitigation.
- Multi-antenna advantage is mainly for phase-difference/CSI-ratio methods (FarSense) and AoA — architecturally impossible for Phantom Scan's single-antenna nodes, confirming the team's own assessment. The **64-subcarrier dimension is the usable diversity axis** for the CNN.

### Q3 — Genuine micro-motion vs vibration/clutter
- **Preprocessing typically fed to CNN-LSTM:** Hampel filter (outlier removal), DC/stationary removal, PCA (motion-component extraction), Butterworth band-pass (respiration 0.1–0.5 Hz; heart 0.8–2.0 Hz), STFT spectrograms, continuous wavelet transform (MF-ABLSTM), and CSI-ratio/conjugate-multiplication for phase-noise cancellation (multi-antenna only).
- **Vibration/non-human separation:** ACF/motion-statistic features (Origin Wireless lineage) cleanly separate periodic fan motion; robot vacuums often fall below the detection threshold; pets are the residual hard case. "Et Tu Alexa?" (arXiv 1810.10109) found server/standing fans didn't affect the amplitude-CSI motion statistic, an oscillating fan did but was separable by its periodicity, and cats/dogs were NOT reliably separable. Real-world scaling data (arXiv 2506.04322, a 2-year, 280-edge-device deployment) reduced "false alarms due to non-human movements from 63.1% to 8.4%" — evidence the problem is tractable but never fully eliminated.
- **Design implication:** For Phantom Scan's documented mechanical-vibration false-trigger risk, a periodicity/ACF feature plus a learned classifier is the evidence-based route; the sovereign verdict should require corroborating non-periodic, broadband spectral signatures before a human call.

### Q4 — Through-wall accuracy & driving factors
- Commodity through-wall HAR: ~90–97.5% in-domain; ESP32-S3 through-wall CNN feasibility demonstrated on a small released dataset. Sampling-rate sensitivity: Widar3.0 lost only ~4% dropping to 250 Hz and stayed >85%; single-link vital work found ~10 Hz optimal for respiration (respiration MAE ~0.2 BPM, heart-rate MAE ~0.6 BPM at 10 Hz in one study). **Phantom Scan's ~41–43 packets/s is adequate for respiration (needs ≥~10 Hz) and gross motion, but marginal for reliable heart rate.**

### Q5 — Compute/latency & TFLM feasibility
- Compact CNN-LSTM inference is single-digit-to-tens of ms on a PC/edge CPU; INT8 DeepConvLSTM = 136.51 KB, 21 ms on MCU-class hardware.
- ESP32-S3: ~512 KB SRAM (+PSRAM), Xtensa LX7 dual-core @240 MHz, PIE vector acceleration for MAC ops; TFLM supports `UNIDIRECTIONAL_SEQUENCE_LSTM` since Sept 2022; INT8 quantization cuts size 4× and time 2–4× at ~1–3% accuracy loss. But an unrolled LSTM needing ~398 KB RAM failed on a 256 KB board — margins are tight.
- **Verdict:** PC/edge server hosts the CNN-LSTM (real-time, easy). On-node, only a trivially small 1D-CNN or single-layer GRU motion gate is realistic — and even that competes with the existing cheap amplitude-variance heuristic.

### Q6 — Comparable two-node / single-antenna systems
- **Pulse-Fi:** two single-antenna ESP32s — closest *hardware* analog.
- **Wi-MoID / SrcSense / "Who Moved My Cheese?":** single pair of transceivers, through-wall, human-vs-non-human — closest *task* analog for false-trigger rejection.
- **Dilution-effect study (arXiv 2602.10823):** single well-placed link beat 72-link fusion; naive high-dimensional feature concatenation hurt. Also: at 1.4 Hz sampling, ambulatory activities hit 100% detection but sedentary only 38% — a warning that static/seated humans are hard.
- **Implication for the two-node plan:** fuse at the *decision/feature-selection* level, not by naive concatenation; treat the second node primarily as coverage/redundancy, not a guaranteed accuracy multiplier.

### Q7 — Training data, augmentation, generalization
- Cross-subject and cross-environment generalization are the dominant failure modes; random splits inflate results 10–30+ points.
- Proven CSI mitigations: temporal-window shift, time-warping, amplitude scaling, Gaussian-noise injection, EMD augmentation (59.91%→97.55% in one embedded study), GAN synthesis (CsiGAN, CSI4Free), diffusion augmentation, contrastive/few-shot learning, transfer learning.
- For a small Jordan-based academic team: collect subject- and room-diverse data from day one; hold out whole subjects and whole rooms for test; use augmentation + transfer learning; publish confusion matrices and empty-room false-alarm rates.

### Q8 — Concrete recommended pipeline for Phantom Scan
**Input tensor.** Per node: a window of T packets × 64 subcarriers of amplitude (optionally + sanitized relative phase). At ~42 pkt/s, a 5 s window ≈ 210 packets → resample to a fixed 256 (or 128) time steps. Two nodes → either two parallel input branches [256 × 64] fused after the CNN stage, or a [256 × 64 × 2] tensor (2 = node channels). Add engineered feature channels: per-subcarrier amplitude variance (the existing heuristic) and ACF/motion-statistic + periodicity features (for vibration rejection).

**Preprocessing.** Hampel outlier filter → DC/stationary removal → subcarrier selection/normalization (NBVI-style) → optional PCA denoise → task-specific band-pass (motion vs respiration bands) → optional STFT-spectrogram branch for a CNN image head.

**Network.** Compact CNN-GRU/CNN-LSTM: 2–3 Conv1D layers (e.g., 32→64→64 filters, kernel 3–5, BatchNorm, ReLU, MaxPool) extracting subcarrier-spatial features per time step → 1 GRU or LSTM layer (64–128 hidden units) for temporal dynamics → optional attention pooling → dense → sigmoid (presence probability) and/or softmax (human / non-human / no-motion). Keep it small (<200k params) for edge latency and to limit overfitting on a small dataset.

**Training.** Subject- and room-separated splits; heavy augmentation (window shift, time-warp, amplitude scale, Gaussian noise, EMD); class weighting for rare events; early stopping; Adam @1e-3 with LR halving on plateau. Consider transfer learning from a UT-HAR/NTU-Fi/SenseFi-pretrained backbone.

**Integration with the sovereign `is_human` verdict.** The model outputs (presence_probability, motion_class, confidence). This confidence scalar **only adjusts** the amplitude-variance heuristic's confidence — it never solely determines the human/non-human decision. Concretely: the physics-based verdict (variance threshold + periodicity/ACF vibration check + static-object immunity) remains the gate; the CNN-LSTM confidence can raise or lower a corroboration score within bounded limits; a full "human" verdict requires both the heuristic AND the model to agree above threshold (reducing false triggers), while either alone can raise a lower-confidence "possible" alert to the SAR/GCS operator. This matches the evidence that ML alone over-claims and that physics-based ACF features are more robust across environments.

## Recommendations
1. **Phase 1 (now):** Keep the amplitude-variance heuristic as the sovereign gate. Add ACF/periodicity features to reject mechanical-vibration false triggers *before* introducing deep learning — this addresses the documented open risk with the least effort and highest cross-environment robustness (evidence: Origin Wireless lineage; "Et Tu Alexa?").
2. **Phase 1.5:** Collect a properly labeled dataset with ≥8–10 subjects across ≥3 rooms/wall types, with ground truth (camera + respiration belt/pulse-ox). Explicitly log empty-room and fan/vibration/pet sessions. This dataset is the binding constraint on everything downstream.
3. **Phase 2:** Train a compact CNN-GRU/CNN-LSTM (<200k params) on the PC for the binary presence + human/non-human task first (not activities). Report subject- and room-separated accuracy, confusion matrices, and false-alarm rates. **Benchmark to beat:** the existing heuristic's false-alarm rate and >90% cross-subject presence accuracy through drywall.
4. **Phase 3:** Only if Phase 2 clears >90% cross-subject and materially beats the heuristic, wire the model's confidence scalar into the sovereign verdict as a bounded adjustment. Add respiration estimation (Butterworth 0.1–0.5 Hz + FFT/LSTM, Pulse-Fi-style) targeting ±2 BPM in near-LoS; treat heart rate (±5 BPM) and multi-target classification as research-grade stretch goals, not deliverables.
5. **Do not deploy the model on the ESP32-S3** beyond a trivial motion gate; host inference on the PC/edge GCS. Revisit TFLM-on-node only if a <150 KB INT8 model and untethered operation become hard requirements.
6. **Thresholds that change the plan:** if cross-subject accuracy stays <85% or the cross-room drop exceeds ~20 points after augmentation, pivot to physics-based ACF features + per-deployment few-shot calibration rather than a general deep model; if the second node yields no measurable fusion gain (dilution effect), treat it as coverage redundancy, not an accuracy multiplier.

## Caveats
- **Marketing vs peer-reviewed:** Several high-visibility "see-through-wall / vitals from WiFi" claims (RuView/ruview.pro, the wifi-densepose GitHub, RuVector) are self-published project/marketing material, not peer-reviewed; their drywall/vital-sign claims should be treated as unverified. The RuView docs themselves concede single-ESP32 deployments have "limited spatial resolution" and pose estimation "has obvious accuracy limits."
- **Inflated accuracy:** Peer-reviewed 98–99% CSI-HAR figures are frequently the product of subject-mixed random splits (documented leakage in the Varga Sensors/Signals/Inventions papers). Realistic cross-subject/cross-environment numbers are meaningfully lower.
- **ESP32 CSI quality:** Multiple sources note ESP32 CSI phase is unreliable and amplitude noisier than Intel 5300 NIC data behind most cited high-accuracy work; one ESP32 fall-detection replication couldn't cleanly separate activity from noise. Phantom Scan's numbers will likely trail NIC-based literature.
- **Sampling rate:** ~41–43 packets/s supports gross motion and respiration but is marginal for reliable heart-rate extraction (literature favors ≥10 Hz clean data with careful filtering, and heart-rate work often uses higher rates).
- **Static/seated humans and pets** remain the hardest cases across the literature; a "no detection" must never be reported as "no human present."
- **Single-study claims** (STC-NLSTMNet 99.88%; some 100% fall-detection results on tiny datasets; SrcSense's 99.77% only after 20 s accumulation) should be treated as preliminary until independently reproduced with proper subject/environment splits.
